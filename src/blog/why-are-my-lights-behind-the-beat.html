Why are my lights behind the beat?

You hit play, the bass drops, and the lights flash a moment later. It feels small, but your eyes catch it every time. The good news: it is almost always one of three things, and most of them are free to fix.

  • The speaker is late. Bluetooth speakers play the sound a little after your device sends it. The lights react on time, the sound arrives late, and it looks like the lights are off.
  • The Wi-Fi is busy. Smart lights get their orders over Wi-Fi. A weak signal or a crowded network slows them down or makes them stutter.
  • The mic is guessing. Many light apps listen through a microphone. The mic has to hear the music across the room, and it hears everything else too.

Is my Bluetooth speaker causing the delay?

This is the most common one, and the easiest to test. Play a song with a hard, simple kick drum and watch one light. If the flash seems to land just before the kick, your speaker is running late.

  • Try a cable. Plug a speaker into the headphone jack or a USB sound card. Wired sound plays right away.
  • Look for a delay setting. Many light apps have a sync or offset slider. Slide it until the kick and the flash land together.
  • Mind the radio. On many laptops, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi share the same radio. Sending music over Bluetooth can make your Wi-Fi lights answer slower too. One more reason to try a cable.

How do I fix Wi-Fi lag on my lights?

Lights that freeze, skip or drift behind usually have a Wi-Fi problem, not a music problem.

  1. Use 2.4 GHz. Many smart lights only join 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi. Keep them on your main network, not a guest network.
  2. Give them a clear path. Walls, mirrors, metal and fish tanks all eat signal. If the curtain's controller box can see the router, or close to it, it will keep up better.
  3. Move the router or add an extender. One light lagging alone usually means it is too far away.
  4. Pick a clean channel. If several lights drop out at the same moment, look at the router. Setting the 2.4 GHz channel to 1, 6 or 11 often helps.
  5. Lighten the load. A 4K stream, a game download and three video calls on the same Wi-Fi all compete with your lights. Pause the big stuff during the party.
Tip. Test one thing at a time. Swap the speaker first, then move the router. If you change five things at once, you never learn which one fixed it.

Why does the mic make my lights feel late or random?

A microphone hears the room, not the song. It has to wait for the sound to travel to it, and it picks up talking, clapping and the dog. That is why many brands tell you to keep the mic close to the speaker. If your lights live across the room, the show will always feel a little loose.
