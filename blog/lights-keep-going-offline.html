Why do my smart lights keep going offline? Wi-Fi fixes that work

By Graham Lucht, founder of GlowBop · October 27, 2026

The short answer

Most smart lights that keep going offline have a Wi-Fi problem, not a broken light: they only join 2.4 GHz, they sit far from the router, or the 2.4 GHz channel is crowded. Set each light up in its own app first, keep it on your main Wi-Fi (not the guest network), move the controller closer to the router, and cable your speaker instead of using Bluetooth. GlowBop adds a Check my lights button that tells you in plain words what is wrong.

Why do my smart lights keep going offline?

When a light drops off, it is easy to blame the light. Most of the time it is the Wi-Fi around it. Smart lights have small radios, they often live outside or behind a TV, and they talk on the busiest band in your house. Here are the fixes that help most, in the order I would try them. None of them need a special app.

Why won't my lights connect at setup?

  • Use 2.4 GHz. Many smart lights only join 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi. Twinkly's router tips say so, for example. If your router mixes 2.4 and 5 GHz under one name, check its settings for a way to split them or turn on a 2.4 GHz network for smart devices.
  • Mesh routers. Mesh systems are great for phones but can confuse some lights during setup. Stand near the main unit when you set the light up, and check your mesh app for a smart home or 2.4 GHz option.
  • No guest network. Guest networks often block devices from seeing each other. Put the lights, your computer and your phone on your main Wi-Fi.
  • Turn off the VPN on your phone. A VPN can stop your phone from seeing devices on your home network.
  • Set it up in its own app first. Get the light working in the Twinkly, Nanoleaf or LIFX app before anything else. If it does not work there, nothing else will see it either.

Why do my lights drop off after they were working?

  • Line of sight to the router. The controller box is the part that talks to Wi-Fi, not the bulbs. Move the box (or the router) so there are fewer walls, doors and appliances between them. An extender near the lights helps a lot.
  • Signal strength. If your light's app or your router shows signal strength, look at it. A weak signal leads to missed commands and lights that freeze mid show.
  • A crowded channel. If several lights drop at the same moment, the router is the likely cause. In your router settings, try setting the 2.4 GHz channel by hand to 1, 6 or 11, with a 20 MHz channel width.
  • Bluetooth speakers. Bluetooth and 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi share the same air. If your computer plays music over a Bluetooth speaker, the lights on that same computer can slow down. Cable the speaker if you can.
  • Restart in the right order. Unplug the light for 10 seconds, plug it back in, then look for it again.
Tip. One light dropping out alone usually means that light needs to be closer to the router. Lots of lights dropping out together usually means the router or channel.

Questions people ask

Why won't my smart lights connect to my Wi-Fi at setup?

Many smart lights only join 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi, and some routers hide that band behind one combined network name. Check your light's help pages, use your main network instead of the guest network, and turn off any VPN on the phone you use for setup.

Why do my lights keep dropping off Wi-Fi?

Usually the light is too far from the router, a wall is in the way, or the 2.4 GHz channel is crowded by neighbours. If several lights drop at the same moment, the router is the likely cause, not the lights.

Can a Bluetooth speaker make my Wi-Fi lights worse?

Yes. Bluetooth and 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi share the same radio space, so a computer playing over Bluetooth can slow its Wi-Fi lights down. A cable to the speaker fixes it.

Do my phone and lights need to be on the same Wi-Fi as GlowBop?

Yes. The lights, the computer running GlowBop and your phone all join the same network. Away from home, connect the laptop to your phone's hotspot instead.

What does Your lights are taking a breather mean in GlowBop?

It means your phone cannot reach the GlowBop laptop right now. Check the same Wi-Fi, that the laptop is awake and that GlowBop is open; it reconnects by itself as soon as the laptop answers.

Should I set my lights up in their own app first?

Yes. Get each light working in its maker's app first, so you know it is on Wi-Fi and healthy. Then GlowBop can find it and take over.
